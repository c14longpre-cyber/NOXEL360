What Is CRO (Conversion Rate Optimization)?

In one sentence: CRO is the practice of improving a website or page so that a higher percentage of visitors complete a desired action — a purchase, a sign-up, a form submission — without needing to drive any additional traffic.

Driving traffic to a website is only half the equation. CRO focuses on the other half: what happens once someone actually arrives, and how many of them follow through on the action you actually want them to take.

What "Conversion" Actually Means

A conversion is simply any completed action that matters to a business — a purchase, a newsletter sign-up, a demo request, a phone call. Conversion rate is the percentage of visitors who complete that action out of everyone who visited.

Why CRO Can Deliver Results Faster Than More Traffic

Doubling a site's traffic is often expensive and slow. Doubling a site's conversion rate — through better copy, clearer calls-to-action, reduced friction in a checkout process — can sometimes be achieved much faster and at lower cost, with the same amount of traffic converting significantly better.

Common CRO Improvements

  • Clearer, more compelling calls-to-action
  • Reducing unnecessary steps in a checkout or sign-up process
  • Building trust signals (reviews, guarantees, security badges) near key decision points
  • Improving page load speed (slow pages measurably hurt conversions)
  • Making forms shorter and less intimidating
  • Testing different headlines, images, or layouts

CRO Relies on Testing, Not Guessing

Effective CRO isn't about assuming what will work — it's about testing changes and measuring actual impact on real visitor behavior. This is where A/B testing (covered separately) becomes central to doing CRO properly, rather than just making intuitive guesses about what might convert better.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is CRO more important than driving more traffic?

Neither is universally more important — they solve different problems. If a site converts well but has low traffic, more traffic helps most. If traffic is high but conversions are poor, CRO often delivers faster, cheaper results.

What's a good conversion rate?

It varies enormously by industry and goal type — a strong e-commerce conversion rate might be 2-3%, while a strong lead-generation form might convert at 10% or more. Comparing against your own baseline matters more than a universal benchmark.

Do I need a lot of traffic to do CRO effectively?

Formal statistical testing (like A/B testing) benefits from higher traffic to reach reliable conclusions faster, but basic CRO principles — clearer calls-to-action, reduced friction — help regardless of traffic volume.
