What Is Event-Driven Automation?

In one sentence: Event-driven automation reacts instantly the moment something specific happens, rather than checking for changes on a fixed schedule.

There are two fundamentally different ways an automated system can know it's time to act: check periodically ("is anything new every 5 minutes?") or get notified the instant something happens. Event-driven automation is the second approach — and it's usually far more efficient.

Scheduled vs Event-Driven: A Simple Comparison

Scheduled automation checks for changes at fixed intervals — every hour, every day. If something happens right after a check, it won't be noticed until the next scheduled check runs, creating a built-in delay.

Event-driven automation reacts the instant something happens — no waiting for the next check, no built-in delay. The moment an event occurs, the automation fires immediately.

A Practical Example

Imagine wanting to notify your team the moment a customer submits a support request. With scheduled automation, you might check for new requests every 15 minutes — meaning a customer could wait up to 15 minutes before anyone even knows they reached out. With event-driven automation, the notification fires the instant the form is submitted — genuinely real-time.

Webhooks: The Technology Behind Most Event-Driven Automation

Webhooks (covered in more depth separately) are the most common mechanism enabling event-driven automation — one system automatically sends a message to another the moment a specific event occurs, without either system needing to constantly ask "did anything happen yet?"

When Event-Driven Automation Matters Most

  • Time-sensitive customer interactions (support requests, order confirmations)
  • Fraud or security alerts that need immediate response
  • Real-time inventory or availability updates
  • Any workflow where a delay would genuinely hurt the experience

For less time-sensitive tasks — like a weekly summary report — scheduled automation is often simpler and perfectly adequate; event-driven automation earns its complexity specifically when timing really matters.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the alternative to event-driven automation?

Scheduled (or "polling") automation, which checks for changes at fixed intervals — every hour, every day — rather than reacting the instant something happens.

Is event-driven automation always better than scheduled automation?

Not always — for tasks that don't need instant reaction (like a daily summary report), scheduled automation is simpler and perfectly sufficient. Event-driven automation shines specifically when speed matters.

What technology enables event-driven automation?

Webhooks are the most common mechanism — a system automatically sends a notification the instant something happens, which other systems can react to immediately.
