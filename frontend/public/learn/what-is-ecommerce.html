What Is E-commerce? A Simple Explanation

In one sentence: E-commerce is the buying and selling of goods or services over the internet, covering everything from a small online store to large marketplace platforms.

E-commerce has grown from a niche alternative to physical retail into a massive, standard part of how people shop — understanding its basic building blocks helps make sense of everything from platform choice to payment processing.

Common E-commerce Business Models

  • B2C (Business-to-Consumer) — selling directly to individual shoppers
  • B2B (Business-to-Business) — selling to other businesses, often at higher volumes with different pricing structures
  • C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer) — individuals selling to other individuals, typically through a marketplace platform
  • Dropshipping — selling products without holding inventory, with a third party fulfilling and shipping orders directly

The Core Components of an E-commerce Business

  • A storefront (website or marketplace listing) where customers browse and buy
  • A payment gateway to securely process transactions
  • Inventory management to track stock
  • Shipping and fulfillment to get products to customers
  • Customer accounts and order history

Popular E-commerce Platforms

Platforms like Shopify, WooCommerce (a WordPress plugin), and Magento handle most of the technical complexity of running an online store, letting business owners focus on products and marketing rather than building infrastructure from scratch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need coding skills to start an e-commerce store?

No. Platforms like Shopify are specifically designed to let non-developers launch a fully functional online store without writing code.

What's the difference between B2C and B2B e-commerce?

B2C (business-to-consumer) sells directly to individual shoppers. B2B (business-to-business) sells to other businesses, often involving different pricing, ordering processes, and larger order volumes.

Is dropshipping a type of e-commerce?

Yes — dropshipping is an e-commerce business model where the seller doesn't hold inventory directly, instead having a third party ship products straight to the customer.
