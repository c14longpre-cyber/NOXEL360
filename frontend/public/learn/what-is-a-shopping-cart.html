What Is a Shopping Cart? A Simple Explanation

🕐 4 min read 📊 Beginner 📁 E-commerce 📅 Published July 24, 2026 🔄 Updated July 24, 2026
On This Page

  • FAQ
In one sentence: A shopping cart is the feature on an online store that lets a visitor collect items they intend to buy, holding them until checkout, similar to a physical cart in a store.

The shopping cart is one of the oldest and most familiar e-commerce concepts, but a surprising amount happens behind the scenes to make it work reliably.

How a Shopping Cart Actually Works

Behind the scenes, a cart tracks selected items, quantities, and prices, tied to a specific session or logged-in account, until the customer either completes checkout or the session expires.

  • Baymard Institute — Cart Abandonment Research · widely cited abandonment rate research

Cart Abandonment: A Persistent E-commerce Challenge

A large share of shopping carts are abandoned before purchase — due to unexpected shipping costs, a required account creation, a confusing checkout, or simply comparison shopping without intent to buy yet.

💡 Did You Know?

Cart abandonment rates across e-commerce average well above 60-70% industry-wide — meaning most people who add something to a cart never actually complete the purchase.

Common Ways to Reduce Cart Abandonment

  • Showing shipping costs early, not as a checkout surprise
  • Offering guest checkout instead of forcing account creation
  • Keeping the checkout process short and simple
  • Sending abandoned cart recovery emails
  • Displaying trust signals near the payment step

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do so many shopping carts get abandoned?

Common reasons include unexpected costs, forced account creation, a complicated checkout, or simply browsing without immediate purchase intent.

What is an abandoned cart email?

A follow-up email automatically sent to a visitor who added items to their cart but didn't complete checkout, often including a reminder or incentive.

Does a shopping cart save items if I close my browser?

It depends on the store's setup — many persist cart contents via cookies or account login, though guest sessions can sometimes expire.

Is guest checkout better than requiring an account?

Generally yes for conversion — forcing account creation is a commonly cited cause of cart abandonment.

Can shopping cart software integrate with inventory systems?

Yes — most modern e-commerce platforms sync cart activity directly with inventory to prevent overselling out-of-stock items.

What's a normal cart abandonment rate?

Industry research commonly finds rates well above 60%, though this varies by industry, device type, and checkout design.

Key Takeaways

  • A shopping cart holds selected items until a customer completes checkout.
  • Cart contents are tracked via a session or logged-in account behind the scenes.
  • Cart abandonment is a persistent, common e-commerce challenge.
  • Unexpected costs and forced account creation are common abandonment causes.
  • Guest checkout and recovery emails are common ways to reduce abandonment.
