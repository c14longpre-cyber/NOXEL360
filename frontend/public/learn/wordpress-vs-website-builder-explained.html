Should I Use WordPress or a Website Builder?

🕐 5 min read 📊 Beginner 📁 Business Questions 📅 Published July 24, 2026 🔄 Updated July 24, 2026
On This Page
  • Side-by-Side Comparison
  • A Simple Way to Decide
  • A Consideration Often Overlooked: Maintenance
  • FAQ
In one sentence: For most small businesses wanting simplicity, a hosted website builder (Squarespace, Wix, Shopify) is easier; for those wanting maximum flexibility and control, WordPress offers more power at the cost of a steeper learning curve.

Both are legitimate, widely used choices — the right answer depends on how much control and flexibility you actually need versus how much simplicity you'd prefer.

💡 Did You Know?

WordPress powers a substantial share of all websites on the internet, while hosted builders like Squarespace and Wix have grown rapidly by prioritizing simplicity over WordPress's greater flexibility.

A Simple Way to Decide

If you want to be up and running quickly with minimal technical involvement, a website builder is usually the better fit. If you anticipate needing significant customization or plan to grow into more complex functionality, WordPress's flexibility becomes more valuable.

A Consideration Often Overlooked: Maintenance

WordPress requires ongoing software updates and security maintenance that a hosted builder handles automatically — a real ongoing responsibility to factor in, not just an upfront setup decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is WordPress harder to use than Squarespace or Wix?

Generally yes — WordPress has a steeper learning curve, though it offers significantly more flexibility and customization in return.

Does WordPress require separate hosting?

Yes, self-hosted WordPress requires you to arrange and manage hosting separately, unlike most all-in-one website builders.

Can I switch from a website builder to WordPress later?

Yes, though it typically requires rebuilding the site, since content and design don't transfer directly between different platforms.

Is WordPress free?

The WordPress software itself is free and open-source, though hosting, premium themes, and plugins often involve real costs.

Which is better for e-commerce, WordPress or a website builder?

Both support e-commerce well — Shopify (a builder) is purpose-built for it, while WordPress with WooCommerce offers similar capability with more customization.

Do I need technical knowledge to maintain a WordPress site?

Some ongoing technical awareness helps, particularly for updates and security, though many businesses hire help specifically for this maintenance.

Key Takeaways

  • Website builders prioritize simplicity; WordPress prioritizes flexibility.
  • WordPress requires separate hosting and more ongoing maintenance.
  • The right choice depends on customization needs versus ease of use preference.
  • Both support e-commerce, with different platforms specializing differently.
  • Switching platforms later typically requires a rebuild, not a simple transfer.
