What Is a Marketplace? A Simple Explanation

🕐 5 min read 📊 Beginner 📁 E-commerce 📅 Published July 24, 2026 🔄 Updated July 24, 2026
On This Page

  • Understanding Marketplace Fees

In one sentence: An online marketplace is a platform where many different sellers list and sell products, with the marketplace itself handling traffic, discovery, and often payments — like Amazon, Etsy, or eBay.

A marketplace and your own online store solve a similar problem — selling products online — but with very different trade-offs around control, visibility, and cost.

Marketplace vs Your Own Store

Marketplace advantages: built-in buyer traffic, established trust, less setup effort.

Marketplace disadvantages: significant fees, limited branding control, direct competition on the same page, no ownership of the customer relationship.

📚 Official Sources
  • BigCommerce — Marketplace vs Own Store · a platform comparison overview
💡 Did You Know?

Some marketplaces host millions of active sellers simultaneously, meaning a huge amount of built-in buyer traffic exists — but so does an enormous amount of direct competition for visibility.

Understanding Marketplace Fees

Marketplaces typically charge a percentage of each sale, sometimes alongside listing or subscription fees — these costs need to be factored directly into product pricing and margins.

Many Businesses Use Both

A common strategy is selling on a marketplace for reach and discovery, while also running an owned store for better margins, branding, and direct customer relationships.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is selling on a marketplace better than having my own store?

Neither is universally better — marketplaces offer built-in traffic but less control; your own store offers more control but requires driving your own traffic.

What fees do marketplaces typically charge?

Usually a percentage of each sale, sometimes combined with listing or subscription fees — the exact structure varies by platform.

Can I sell on a marketplace and have my own store at the same time?

Yes — this hybrid approach is common, using the marketplace for reach while building an owned customer base separately.

Do I own the customer relationship when selling on a marketplace?

Generally not directly — many marketplaces limit direct access to customer contact information, keeping that relationship with the platform.

Are marketplace listings affected by SEO?

Yes, within the marketplace's own search — optimizing product titles and descriptions for the marketplace's internal search matters, separate from Google SEO.

What's an example of a marketplace versus an independent store platform?

Amazon and Etsy are marketplaces; Shopify and WooCommerce are platforms for building an independent, owned store.

Key Takeaways

  • A marketplace hosts many sellers, providing built-in traffic and trust.
  • Marketplaces charge fees and limit branding and customer-relationship control.
  • An owned store offers more control but requires driving your own traffic.
  • Many businesses use both a marketplace and an owned store together.
  • Marketplace fees must be factored directly into product pricing.
