What Is Process Automation? A Simple Explanation

In one sentence: Process automation applies automation to an entire, well-defined business process end-to-end, rather than just one isolated task or connection between two tools.

Process automation and workflow automation get used almost interchangeably, but there's a useful distinction: workflow automation often connects individual steps between tools; process automation takes a wider view of an entire business process, from start to finish.

A Process Automation Example

Consider an employee onboarding process: collecting documents, setting up accounts, scheduling training, assigning equipment, and notifying relevant teams. Process automation maps out that entire sequence and automates as much of it as realistically possible — not just one isolated handoff, but the complete journey from "new hire accepted" to "fully onboarded."

Why Businesses Formally Map Processes First

Before automating a process, it usually needs to be clearly mapped out — every step, every decision point, every handoff. This mapping exercise alone often reveals inefficiencies even before any automation is built, since it forces a business to actually document what currently happens, which is sometimes less consistent than assumed.

Common Business Processes Worth Automating

  • Employee onboarding and offboarding
  • Invoice approval and payment processing
  • Customer support ticket routing
  • Lead qualification and follow-up
  • Inventory reordering
  • Content approval and publishing

When Process Automation Makes the Most Sense

The best candidates are processes that happen frequently, follow a fairly consistent pattern, and don't require significant human judgment at every step. Rare, highly variable, or judgment-heavy processes are usually poor automation candidates — trying to force them into a rigid automated flow often creates more problems than it solves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between process automation and workflow automation?

Workflow automation typically connects individual tools together for a specific sequence. Process automation takes a broader view, automating an entire business process end-to-end, which may include multiple workflows.

Is process automation expensive to implement?

It varies widely — from free or low-cost no-code tools for simple processes, to significant investment for large, complex enterprise process automation projects.

Should every business process be automated?

No. Processes that are rare, highly variable, or require significant human judgment often aren't good automation candidates — the best targets are frequent, well-defined, repeatable processes.
