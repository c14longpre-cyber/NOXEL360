What Are KPIs? A Simple Explanation

In one sentence: A KPI (Key Performance Indicator) is a specific, measurable value that tracks progress toward a defined business goal, chosen deliberately from among many possible metrics available.

Businesses can track hundreds of possible metrics. A KPI is different — it's a small, deliberately chosen set that actually reflects whether the business is succeeding at something that matters.

Not Every Metric Is a KPI

A metric is simply anything measurable. A KPI is a metric specifically chosen because it directly reflects progress on a real business goal — deliberately narrowed down, not everything tracked at once.

💡 Did You Know?

A common mistake is tracking dozens of metrics as if they were all KPIs — genuine KPIs are meant to be few and focused, since tracking everything as equally important tends to dilute actual decision-making.

What Makes a Good KPI

  • Relevant — directly tied to a real business goal
  • Measurable — a clear, trackable number
  • Actionable — something the business can actually influence
  • Time-bound — tracked over a defined period

Frequently Asked Questions

How many KPIs should a business track?

Fewer than it might seem — a small, focused set (often 3-5 per goal) tends to drive clearer decisions than tracking dozens of metrics equally.

Is revenue always a good KPI?

It's a common one, but often too broad on its own — more specific KPIs (like customer acquisition cost) help explain why revenue is moving.

Can KPIs change over time?

Yes — as business priorities shift, the specific KPIs tracked should shift too, rather than staying fixed indefinitely.

What's the difference between a KPI and a target?

A KPI is what's being measured. A target is the specific goal value set for that KPI over a given period.

Do different departments need different KPIs?

Generally yes — marketing, sales, and product teams typically track different KPIs relevant to their specific goals, even within one business.

Can a business have too many KPIs?

Yes — tracking too many dilutes focus and can make it harder to identify what's actually driving results.

Should KPIs be reviewed regularly?

Yes — periodic review ensures the KPIs still reflect current priorities and that data collection remains accurate.

Key Takeaways

  • A KPI is a deliberately chosen metric tracking progress toward a real business goal.
  • Not every measurable metric qualifies as a genuine KPI.
  • Good KPIs are relevant, measurable, actionable, and time-bound.
  • Different business areas typically track different, specific KPIs.
  • Fewer, focused KPIs tend to drive clearer decisions than tracking everything.
