What Is A/B Testing? A Simple Explanation

🕐 5 min read 📊 Intermediate 📁 Digital Marketing 📅 Published July 24, 2026 🔄 Updated July 24, 2026

  • What Gets A/B Tested

In one sentence: A/B testing compares two versions of a page or element — showing each to a portion of real visitors — to measure, with actual data, which one performs better on a specific goal.

Instead of guessing which headline, button color, or layout will perform better, A/B testing lets real visitor behavior settle the question directly, with actual numbers rather than opinions.

How an A/B Test Actually Works

  1. Create two versions — "A" (original) and "B" (variation)
  2. Show each to a separate portion of real traffic, randomly split
  3. Track a specific goal for each version
  4. Compare results after enough data accumulates
💡 Did You Know?

Some of the world's largest tech companies run thousands of simultaneous A/B tests at any given time, treating nearly every product decision as a testable hypothesis rather than an assumption.

What Gets A/B Tested

  • Headlines and copy
  • Call-to-action button text, color, or placement
  • Page layouts
  • Images or visuals
  • Pricing displays
  • Email subject lines

Why 'Statistical Significance' Actually Matters

A small difference after a handful of visitors could easily be random chance. Statistical significance checks whether an observed difference is large and consistent enough to trust as real.

CRO is the overall goal — improving how well a page converts. A/B testing is one of the primary tools used to actually validate whether a proposed CRO improvement genuinely works.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much traffic do I need to run a valid A/B test?

It depends on the baseline conversion rate and the size of the difference you're trying to detect — low-traffic sites often need longer test periods.

Can I test more than two versions at once?

Yes — testing more than two variants is called multivariate testing, though it typically requires more traffic to reach reliable conclusions.

What happens if a test result isn't statistically significant?

It means there isn't enough confidence yet to declare a winner — the honest conclusion is to keep testing longer or accept it as inconclusive.

How long should an A/B test run?

Long enough to reach statistical significance and account for natural variation like day-of-week patterns — often at least one to two full weeks.

Can A/B testing be used outside of websites?

Yes — email subject lines, ad creative, and even product features are commonly A/B tested using the same core methodology.

What's the risk of stopping a test too early?

Early results can reflect random variation rather than a real effect, leading to a confident but incorrect conclusion about which version is better.

Do small businesses have enough traffic to A/B test?

Lower-traffic sites can still test, though they typically need to run tests longer or focus on higher-impact, more visible changes.

Key Takeaways

  • A/B testing compares two real versions with actual visitor traffic.
  • The process: create variants, split traffic, track a goal, compare results.
  • Statistical significance distinguishes a real effect from random chance.
  • Headlines, CTAs, layouts, and pricing are commonly tested elements.
  • A/B testing is the primary tool for validating whether CRO changes actually work.
